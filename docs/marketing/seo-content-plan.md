## Strategic Frame

Axel's SEO surface area is large but must be approached in tight vertical wedges rather than spraying thin content. The core insight: **nobody searches for "AI employee"** yet — so we own the category by targeting the jobs-to-be-done searches that naturally lead to that conclusion.

Primary audience signal: SMB founders, ops leads, agency owners, solo consultants — people who run process-heavy work without a full admin team. These people search for _pain relief_, not product categories.

**Pillar page:** `/ai-employee` (or blog post → pillar)
Topic: What it means to have an AI employee vs a chatbot. Educates the searcher, positions Axel as the answer.

Supporting content:

- "The difference between an AI chatbot and an AI employee"
- "What an AI employee actually does all day"
- "How to onboard an AI employee into your business"
- "Can an AI employee replace a virtual assistant?"

### Cluster 2: SMB Productivity / Founder Ops

**Pillar page:** `/for-founders` or blog category
Topic: How founders and small business operators can reclaim time and run ops without a team.

Supporting content:

- "How I automated my admin using AI (and what I learned)"
- "The daily founder stack: what to keep, what to hand to AI"
- "Running a 10-person agency without an ops manager"
- "AI tools for agency owners in 2026"

### Cluster 3: Meeting Intelligence

**Pillar page:** `/meeting-notes` or `/meeting-intelligence`
Topic: Automatic notes, action items, follow-ups — the meeting workflow use case.

Supporting content:

- "How to never manually write meeting notes again"
- "AI meeting follow-up: how to automate post-call tasks"
- "Best meeting summary tools compared"
- Comparison: Axel vs Otter vs Fireflies vs Notion AI for meetings

### Cluster 4: Comparisons

Each page captures a comparison search and positions Axel clearly:

- `/blog/axel-vs-notion-ai`
- `/blog/axel-vs-chatgpt-for-business`
- `/blog/axel-vs-microsoft-copilot`
- `/blog/axel-vs-virtual-assistant`
- `/blog/ai-personal-assistant-compared`

Format: honest, specific, not puff. Comparison tables. Clear winner criteria.

### Cluster 5: Vertical Wedges (process-heavy industries)

Each post targets a specific industry/role:

- "AI assistant for agency owners"
- "AI admin for accountants and bookkeepers"
- "AI for freelance consultants"
- "AI for property managers"
- "AI chief of staff for startup founders"

These pages can reuse a template structure: problem → Axel's approach → specific features that apply → CTA.

## Content Format Guidelines

- **Pillar posts:** 1,500–2,500 words. No fluff padding. Each section answers a real question.
- **Comparison posts:** Always include a table. Be honest about where competitors are better — it builds trust.
- **Use case posts:** Lead with pain, not features. "If you spend 2 hours a week on X, read this."
- **Tone:** Smart, direct, human. Same voice as the product. Never "revolutionary AI-powered synergy".
- **CTAs in content:** Every post has one CTA — either "Join waitlist" or "See how Axel works". Never two competing CTAs.
