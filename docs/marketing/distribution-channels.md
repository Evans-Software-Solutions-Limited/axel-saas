## Strategic Frame

SEO is the long game. These channels are the short and medium game — they drive signups, build the brand, and compound SEO authority through backlinks and social signals. Priority is set by effort-to-impact ratio for a small team.

---

## Channel Map

### 1. LinkedIn (High priority — start now)

**Why:** SMB founders, ops leads, and agency owners are active on LinkedIn. This is where the Axel buyer lives. It's also where "AI employee" as a concept can be introduced and owned.

**What to post:**

- Founder content: personal takes on the "AI employee" thesis (Bradley's voice — not corporate, not product marketing)
- Problem posts: "I spent 90 minutes in back-to-back meetings last week and Axel sent the follow-ups before I had lunch" (real usage stories)
- Educational posts: short, specific guides on what an AI employee does that a chatbot can't
- Behind-the-scenes: building Axel, what we're learning, what we're shipping

**Format notes:**

- Native text posts outperform link posts. Put the link in the comments.
- One post per working day is sustainable and enough to build a following. Don't over-produce.
- Engage with replies for the first hour after posting (the algorithm rewards this).

**Cadence:** 3-5 posts per week. Mix personal (Bradley) and product (Axel page if you create one).

**Goal:** 500 followers who are SMB founders / operators within 90 days. These people become waitlist leads.

---

### 2. Founder Communities (High priority — immediate)

These communities have tight feedback loops. One useful post can drive 50-100 waitlist signups.

**Target communities:**

- **Indie Hackers** — post in "Products" section; honest story of building Axel + link to waitlist
- **Product Hunt** — plan a launch day (coordinate timing with a decent email list to support it); target Top 5 of the day
- **Hacker News: Show HN** — "Show HN: Axel — an AI employee for SMBs" — honest framing, link to demo or homepage; respond to every comment for 48 hours
- **Reddit: r/entrepreneur, r/smallbusiness, r/SaaS, r/productivity** — contribute value first, share Axel when genuinely relevant; never drop a link without context
- **Slack/Discord communities:** Ramen Club, Founders Café, Agency owners groups — join first, be useful, share Axel when the time is right

**What not to do:** Don't spam community threads with product links. These communities remember bad actors.

---

### 3. Founder Content (High priority — medium effort)

The "AI employee" category is new. The person who explains it clearly and consistently will own the mind share.

**What this looks like:**

- A founder (Bradley) writing/posting regularly about the thesis: why chatbots aren't enough, what an AI employee actually does, how SMBs should think about AI ops
- Not product marketing — category building. "Axel" appears as the proof point, not the lead.
- Formats: LinkedIn long-form posts, Twitter/X threads, short essays on the Axel blog, guest posts in founder newsletters

**Why this works:** When potential users Google "what is an AI employee for business", they find Bradley's content → they find Axel → they sign up.

**Output target:** 2-3 substantial pieces per week across formats.

---

### 4. Email / Newsletter Distribution

**Why:** Owned channel. Unaffected by algorithm changes.

**What to build:**

- The waitlist list is already an email list — use it. Send a monthly "what we're building + what we're learning" update. Keep it short and personal.
- Consider a lightweight newsletter targeted at SMB founders on AI ops: curated links + one Axel use case per issue. Builds the audience before the product is fully open.
- Integrate UTM tracking so signups from email are attributable.

**Newsletter ideas:**

- "The AI Ops Brief" — weekly, 5 minutes, no fluff. AI tools + tactics for SMB/founders. Axel featured naturally.

---

### 5. Outbound Outreach (Medium priority — once product is stable)

**Target profile:** SMB founder, 5-20 person company, process-heavy (agency, consultancy, property, professional services), currently using a mix of Notion/Slack/Google Workspace.

**Approach:**

- Personalised LinkedIn DMs from Bradley (not a template blast). Reference something specific about their work or company.
- Short ask: "Would love to show you Axel for 15 minutes — you'd be a perfect fit and I'd value your feedback"
- Goal is user research + waitlist signups + eventual early customer, not immediate sale

**Volume:** 10-15 personalised outreaches per week is manageable. Quality > volume.

**What doesn't work:** Mass cold email sequences. They damage the brand at this stage.

---

### 6. Demo / Product Video

**Why:** Axel's differentiation is behavioural — it's hard to understand from text alone. A 2-3 minute demo video showing a real Axel interaction (daily brief → meeting notes → task follow-up) can convert cold traffic that a landing page can't.

**Where to use it:**

- Homepage (above the fold or in a "How it works" section)
- LinkedIn (native video gets far more reach than links)
- Product Hunt launch
- Outbound outreach follow-up

**Format:** Screen recording with voiceover. Real use case, not polished B-roll. Authenticity beats production at this stage.

**See also:** `/docs/logged-out/product-video-brief.md`

---

### 7. Partnerships & Integrations Marketing

**Why:** Axel's value compounds with integrations. Partners who already have the SMB audience can refer users naturally.

**Target partner types:**

- Tools Axel integrates with (Notion, Google Workspace, Slack, etc.) — explore co-marketing, listing in their app directories
- SMB software aggregators: GetApp, Capterra, G2 — create a profile, get initial reviews
- Complementary SaaS tools (not direct competitors): project management, CRM tools — explore content partnerships (shared blog posts, tool comparison posts)
- Freelancer/agency networks: directories, communities — sponsor content or guest-write

**Short-term action:** Get listed on:

- [ ] Product Hunt (launch day + always-on profile)
- [ ] AlternativeTo (category: AI Personal Assistants)
- [ ] Futurepedia / There's An AI For That
- [ ] G2 / Capterra (once enough reviews to matter)

### 8. SEO (Long game — run in parallel)

Covered in full in `/docs/marketing/seo-content-plan.md`. Key integration points with other channels:

- Every blog post should be shared on LinkedIn (link in first comment)
- Every comparison post earns natural backlinks when shared in communities
- Product Hunt and directory listings create authoritative backlinks

---

### 9. Paid (Defer — not yet)

Not recommended at pre-launch / early waitlist stage. Reasons:

- CAC is high when the product is unproven
- Better to use budget on content and outreach (compounding channels)
- Wait until: strong organic signal, clear converting landing page, and understanding of which persona converts

**When to revisit paid:**

- Google Ads on "AI assistant for small business" style terms — once the landing page converts >3%
- LinkedIn Ads targeting job titles (founder, MD, agency owner) — once the product is fully open
