### Hero copy guidance

- Do **not** lead with "AI-powered" or "cutting-edge". Every competitor says this.
- The word "employee" is the differentiator. Lean into it — but back it up immediately with what Axel actually does.
- "24/7" is a real, concrete claim. Use it.
- The secondary CTA should anchor to a demo or use-cases section — not to sign-up. Capture the curious user before pushing them to commit.

## Proof / Trust Blocks

### Before first social proof is available

Use these structural trust signals at launch:

1. **Built by the OpenClaw team** — "Axel is powered by OpenClaw, an open-source AI agent framework. Built to be reliable, not just impressive."
2. **Data privacy line** — "Your data is yours. We don't train on your conversations." _(Only use if accurate — confirm with Bradley before publishing.)_
3. **Hosting / infrastructure** — "Hosted on AWS." _(Only if accurate.)_
4. **Transparent pricing** — "No hidden fees. Cancel any time. No questions asked."
5. **Waitlist framing** — "Opening in stages. We'll email you the moment it's your turn." (turns a weakness into a trust signal)

### Once social proof is available

Priority order:

1. Quotes from real users (founder/SMB profiles preferred — makes the target audience feel seen)
2. Specific metric claims: "Saves me 8 hours a week" beats "changed my workflow"
3. Logo strip for enterprise / known company users
4. Case study snippet: one concrete before/after story

## What NOT to Write

- Don't call Axel "revolutionary", "game-changing", "next-generation", or "seamless"
- Don't open with feature lists — lead with what the user _stops having to do_
- Don't use "AI-powered [noun]" constructions. Just say what it does.
- Don't bury the "not a chatbot" distinction — it's the core point of difference, say it clearly
- Don't write for VCs or tech press. Write for a 10-person agency owner who has never heard of Axel.
