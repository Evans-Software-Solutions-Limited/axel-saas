# Documentation & Reporting Use Case

**Date:** 2026-03-25  
**Status:** Product direction draft

## Why this use case matters

A strong early Axel SaaS use case is not generic "AI chat".
It is practical work support:

- helping professionals document what they are doing
- converting rough notes into clean outputs
- drafting internal reports
- preparing updates, summaries, and handoff notes
- reducing the friction of written knowledge work

This is especially relevant for Bradley's internal/work deployment path, where Axel may be paid for by an employer rather than justified as a consumer novelty.

---

## Core jobs Axel should do here

### Documentation support

- Turn raw notes into structured documents
- Reorganise messy working docs into clear sections
- Convert bullet points into readable internal prose
- Extract actions, risks, owners, blockers, and open questions
- Maintain continuity across ongoing workstreams

### Report writing support

- Produce first-pass drafts from notes and source materials
- Summarise multiple inputs into one coherent update
- Create status reports, decision notes, and handoff summaries
- Highlight progress, blockers, risks, and next steps
- Help transform "what happened" into "what matters"

### Day-to-day work assistance

- Read links, updates, and reference material before the user does
- Prepare briefings for meetings or review sessions
- Summarise email-like or document-heavy inputs
- Reduce context-switching overhead for busy operators

---

## Product promise

The promise is not "best model access".
The promise is:

**Axel helps you turn messy work into clear outputs, consistently.**

For many users, that is more valuable than open-ended chat.

---

## How this should feel

A user should feel that Axel:

- saves them time before they start writing
- improves clarity once they do start
- helps them finish documentation they would otherwise delay
- reduces the pain of converting thinking into presentable written output

This is a practical, paid-for benefit.

---

## Free vs Paid experience

### Free tier

Useful but bounded:

- summaries
- note cleanup
- basic report skeletons
- simple restructuring
- action extraction
- lightweight drafting

The free tier should feel genuinely helpful for low-stakes documentation tasks.

### Paid tiers

Higher-trust support:

- better reasoning and synthesis
- stronger final writing quality
- more reliable outputs
- more nuanced decision framing
- stakeholder-ready report support
- more continuity across longer projects

The upgrade path is not "more tokens".
It is:

- better judgment
- better polish
- better reliability
- better day-to-day partnership

---

## Routing implication

This use case is well-suited to split routing:

### Cheap / free lane

Use for:

- reading sources
- extracting facts
- pulling actions and risks
- turning notes into rough structure
- first-pass drafts
- low-stakes internal cleanup

### Premium lane

Use for:

- final report refinement
- judgment-heavy synthesis
- stakeholder-facing writing quality
- nuanced recommendations
- outputs where trust and polish matter

This is the right model for both product quality and unit economics.

---

## Why this is a strong early production use case

- common pain
- easy to demonstrate
- easy to explain to buyers
- naturally recurring usage
- supports both individual and internal business deployment
- compatible with tiered routing and cost control

It also avoids overpromising on risky autonomous actions too early.

---

## Product design implications

- chat should support note-to-doc and doc-to-summary flows natively
- users should be able to drop in rough notes and get structured output back
- templates for report types may become a major UX win later
- memory/continuity matters because reports often build on prior work
- source traceability becomes important for trust in work outputs

---

## Recommendation

Treat **Documentation & Reporting Assistant** as a first-class Axel SaaS use case in positioning, onboarding, and routing design.

It is concrete, valuable, commercially legible, and highly compatible with cost-controlled model routing.
